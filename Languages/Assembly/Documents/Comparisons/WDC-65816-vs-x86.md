## Design Philosophy

### WDC-65816
> *Grow up gracefully - 6502 to 16-bit without alienating the community*

### x86
> *Software longevity through hardware complexity - cruft carries forward*

**Key Difference:** WDC-65816 approaches computing with a cisc philosophy, while x86 takes a cisc approach.

## Register Architecture

### WDC-65816
16-bit A, X, Y (native), 24-bit addressing, PB/DB bank registers

### x86
8 × 32-bit (EAX-EDX, ESI, EDI, EBP, ESP)

**Comparison:** Both architectures provide a many-register programming model, though the specific organization differs significantly.

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### WDC-65816
- **Strengths:** 6502 heritage, 16-bit power, banked memory, backward compatible
- **Weaknesses:** Complex banking, niche market, soon overshadowed by 68000 and x86

### x86
- **Strengths:** Massive software base, backward compatibility, performance, ecosystem
- **Weaknesses:** Complex decode, legacy baggage, power hungry, security issues from complexity

---

## When to Choose Which

**Choose WDC-65816 when:** power efficiency is paramount, especially for Apple IIGS

**Choose x86 when:** backward compatibility with massive existing software bases is critical

---

## Summary

WDC-65816 and x86 represent different points in the spectrum of processor design. The 16/8-bit WDC-65816 and 32-bit x86 serve different scale requirements. WDC-65816 excels in constrained environments where every transistor counts, while x86 provides the address space and arithmetic range needed for complex modern workloads.
