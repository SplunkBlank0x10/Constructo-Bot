# Z80 vs Z8000

## Overview

This document compares **Z80** and **Z8000**, two distinct assembly architectures with different design goals, historical contexts, and technical approaches.

| Attribute | Z80 | Z8000 |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Bits** | 8 | 16 |
| **Type** | CISC | CISC |
| **Year** | 1976 | 1979 |
| **Creator** | Zilog (Federico Faggin) | Zilog |
| **Endianness** | Little | Big |

---

## Design Philosophy

### Z80
> *Familiar but better - enhance 8080 without breaking compatibility*

### Z8000
> *16-bit done right - more regular than 8086, flexible segmented or flat*

**Key Difference:** Z80 approaches computing with a cisc philosophy, while Z8000 takes a cisc approach. 

---

## Register Architecture

### Z80
A + A', B,C,D,E,H,L + B',C',D',E',H',L', IX, IY, SP, PC

### Z8000
16 × 16-bit (pairable to 32-bit), two stack pointers

**Comparison:** Z80 offers few registers (few-style architecture), while Z8000 provides many registers (many-style architecture). This fundamentally affects how code is structured and optimized.

---

## Notable Features

### Z80
Alternate register sets (EXX), block instructions (LDI/CPI), DRAM refresh, 3 interrupt modes

### Z8000
Segmented (Z8001) or non-segmented (Z8002), string instructions, 23-bit addressing

---

## Historical Lineage

### Z80
8080 → Z80 (enhanced) → Z180 → Z280 → eZ80

### Z8000
Z80 → Z8000 (16-bit) → Z80000 (32-bit, rare) → discontinued

---

## Primary Use Cases

### Z80
ZX Spectrum, MSX, Game Boy, CP/M, embedded, retro computing, education

### Z8000
Industrial control, military, some Unix workstations, embedded

---

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### Z80
- **Strengths:** 8080 compatible, more registers, powerful block ops, cheap, huge ecosystem
- **Weaknesses:** 8-bit limitation, quirkier than 8080, soon eclipsed by 16-bit era

### Z8000
- **Strengths:** Clean design, orthogonal, flexible addressing, good for systems programming
- **Weaknesses:** Late to market, no software ecosystem, x86 won, discontinued

---

## When to Choose Which

**Choose Z80 when:** power efficiency is paramount, especially for ZX Spectrum

**Choose Z8000 when:** your application fits its domain (Industrial control) and you value its specific strengths

---

## Summary

Z80 and Z8000 represent different points in the spectrum of processor design. The 8-bit Z80 and 16-bit Z8000 serve different scale requirements. Z80 excels in constrained environments where every transistor counts, while Z8000 provides the address space and arithmetic range needed for complex modern workloads.
