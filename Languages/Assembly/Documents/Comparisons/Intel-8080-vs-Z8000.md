# Intel-8080 vs Z8000

## Design Philosophy

### Intel-8080
> *The microprocessor as a computer - general purpose, external memory*

### Z8000
> *16-bit done right - more regular than 8086, flexible segmented or flat*

**Key Difference:** Intel-8080 approaches computing with a cisc philosophy, while Z8000 takes a cisc approach. 

---

## Register Architecture

### Intel-8080
7 × 8-bit (A,B,C,D,E,H,L), pairs as 16-bit (BC,DE,HL)

### Z8000
16 × 16-bit (pairable to 32-bit), two stack pointers

**Comparison:** Both architectures provide a many-register programming model, though the specific organization differs significantly.

---

## Notable Features

### Intel-8080
64KB space, 3 power supplies, 5 interrupts, CP/M era, Altair/IMSAI

### Z8000
Segmented (Z8001) or non-segmented (Z8002), string instructions, 23-bit addressing

## Historical Lineage

### Intel-8080
8008 → 8080 → 8085 → 8086 (not binary compatible but design lineage)

### Z8000
Z80 → Z8000 (16-bit) → Z80000 (32-bit, rare) → discontinued

## Primary Use Cases

### Intel-8080
Early personal computers, CP/M systems, embedded, industrial

### Z8000
Industrial control, military, some Unix workstations, embedded

---

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### Intel-8080
- **Strengths:** First widely successful general-purpose CPU, established 8-bit paradigm
- **Weaknesses:** Limited registers, complex support chips, multiple power supplies

### Z8000
- **Strengths:** Clean design, orthogonal, flexible addressing, good for systems programming
- **Weaknesses:** Late to market, no software ecosystem, x86 won, discontinued

## Summary

Intel-8080 and Z8000 represent different points in the spectrum of processor design. The 8-bit Intel-8080 and 16-bit Z8000 serve different scale requirements. Intel-8080 excels in constrained environments where every transistor counts, while Z8000 provides the address space and arithmetic range needed for complex modern workloads.
