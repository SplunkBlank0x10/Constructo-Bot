## Design Philosophy

### WDC-65816
> *Grow up gracefully - 6502 to 16-bit without alienating the community*

### Z8000
> *16-bit done right - more regular than 8086, flexible segmented or flat*

**Key Difference:** WDC-65816 approaches computing with a cisc philosophy, while Z8000 takes a cisc approach.

## Register Architecture

### WDC-65816
16-bit A, X, Y (native), 24-bit addressing, PB/DB bank registers

### Z8000
16 × 16-bit (pairable to 32-bit), two stack pointers

**Comparison:** Both architectures provide a many-register programming model, though the specific organization differs significantly.

### WDC-65816

### Z8000
Segmented (Z8001) or non-segmented (Z8002), string instructions, 23-bit addressing

## Primary Use Cases

### Z8000

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### WDC-65816
- **Strengths:** 6502 heritage, 16-bit power, banked memory, backward compatible
- **Weaknesses:** Complex banking, niche market, soon overshadowed by 68000 and x86

### Z8000
- **Strengths:** Clean design, orthogonal, flexible addressing, good for systems programming
- **Weaknesses:** Late to market, no software ecosystem, x86 won, discontinued

## When to Choose Which

**Choose WDC-65816 when:** power efficiency is paramount, especially for Apple IIGS

**Choose Z8000 when:** your application fits its domain (Industrial control) and you value its specific strengths

## Summary

WDC-65816 and Z8000 represent different points in the spectrum of processor design. The 16/8-bit WDC-65816 and 16-bit Z8000 serve different scale requirements. WDC-65816 excels in constrained environments where every transistor counts, while Z8000 provides the address space and arithmetic range needed for complex modern workloads.
