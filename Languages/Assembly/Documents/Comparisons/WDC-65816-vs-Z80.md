## Design Philosophy

### WDC-65816
> *Grow up gracefully - 6502 to 16-bit without alienating the community*

### Z80
> *Familiar but better - enhance 8080 without breaking compatibility*

**Key Difference:** WDC-65816 approaches computing with a cisc philosophy, while Z80 takes a cisc approach.

## Register Architecture

### WDC-65816
16-bit A, X, Y (native), 24-bit addressing, PB/DB bank registers

### Z80
A + A', B,C,D,E,H,L + B',C',D',E',H',L', IX, IY, SP, PC

**Comparison:** WDC-65816 offers many registers (many-style architecture), while Z80 provides few registers (few-style architecture). This fundamentally affects how code is structured and optimized.

### Z80

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### WDC-65816
- **Strengths:** 6502 heritage, 16-bit power, banked memory, backward compatible
- **Weaknesses:** Complex banking, niche market, soon overshadowed by 68000 and x86

### Z80
- **Strengths:** 8080 compatible, more registers, powerful block ops, cheap, huge ecosystem
- **Weaknesses:** 8-bit limitation, quirkier than 8080, soon eclipsed by 16-bit era

## Summary

WDC-65816 and Z80 represent different points in the spectrum of processor design. The 8-bit Z80 and 16/8-bit WDC-65816 serve different scale requirements. Z80 excels in constrained environments where every transistor counts, while WDC-65816 provides the address space and arithmetic range needed for complex modern workloads.
